/ 0. Overview — the thesis · 60–90 seconds

Do
Stay on /. Do not scroll-race. Let them read the three failure columns.
Say
PitchIn is a readiness system for local service. It turns a town's unmet needs into taskable units, matches them to what residents actually have — skills, trucks, languages — and then rewards the one thing civic life never measures: showing up when you said you would, week after week.
Civic volunteering does not fail on goodwill. It fails on three things.
Point
The three columns, in order.
  1. Supply chain — demand is unstructured, capacity is invisible, matching happens on a group text.
  2. Loop closure — nobody reports the outcome, so effort disappears and people don't come back.
  3. Cadence — service is treated as episodic and heroic. We know how to build a gym habit. We have almost nothing for a civic one.
Say

The claim — the product's one-sentence bet

Volunteering forty hours once and then vanishing is worth less than twenty minutes a week for two years. PitchIn is the first system that says so out loud.
Point
The four anti-gaming lines under “Any metric becomes a target.” Hit them as a list, not a speech:
  • One rep per week — show-rate can't be farmed.
  • The requester verifies the task — not self-report.
  • Rankings are squad-level — no individual glory board.
  • Rewards stay symbolic and local — no gift-card economy.
Say
If we get the metric wrong, we crowd out the reason people showed up. So the reward layer stays thin on purpose.
Do
Click Enter South Park.

The four mechanisms

Keep these in your head. You will not lecture this list. You will show each one on a screen.

Failure Mechanism Where it becomes visible
Supply chain Decompose a need into tasks with time, skill, and materiel. Match against a capability registry. /post, /need/hansen-flood, /registry
Loop closure Requester verifies. Public after-action report on the wall. /need/hansen-flood, /wall
Cadence One weekly rep, fixed time, named partner. Miss and the ask shrinks — it does not guilt. /me, /squad/creek-side
Anti-gaming One-rep cap, requester-only verify, squad-only public rankings, local symbolic rewards. Enforced in the UI on those screens, not just claimed on the landing page

Vocabulary. The name is warm. The interior is operational: reps, squads, quals, surge, sustainment, show-rate, AAR, standing, materiel. Do not translate these into “sessions,” “teams,” “badges,” or “reports.” The tension is the personality.

The South Park rule. Landmarks and businesses carry the TV-show names (Tweek Bros., City Wok, Hell's Pass, the Cows). Residents never do. Every name is an invented Colorado person. Play it straight. If a line would get a laugh, cut it.

/me 4. My Rep

Role: Nora Beckett · Resident. Nora has missed two weeks. The screen opens in KEEP THE CHAIN.

This is the most original mechanic in the product. Read the card copy verbatim. Do not improve it.
Point
The large rep card.
Say

Verbatim from the card

You've missed the last two. That happens. This one is five minutes — enough to keep the chain alive.
Miss a week and the system does not guilt you, count down, or warn you about your streak. It shrinks the ask. Miss two and it offers five minutes so the chain stays alive. Every habit system in fitness knows this. No civic system does it.

Likely questions

Is this a real town / real people?

South Park is the TV town, played straight. Businesses keep the names; every resident is invented. Frozen date, mock data, no backend. Refresh resets.

Why doesn't someone just drive the plow over?

Because the product is diagnosing geography and inventory, not scolding neighbors. Kenosha Pass is 22 minutes from Tarryall. The stall is the demo.

Why not points / badges / a leaderboard?

Any metric becomes a target. Show-rate is capped at one rep a week, verification is requester-only, public rank is squad-only, rewards are coffee and recognition. The reward layer is thin so it cannot become the reason.

What does “standing” get you?

Sustained readiness unlocks sponsorship rights, a vote on the quarterly task menu, the ability to stand up a squad. Earned authority over the system, not points.

Is the decompose step actually AI?

In this prototype, no. It is a staged reveal of the seed's eleven Hansen tasks. The product claim is that decomposition is the hard language problem; the demo must never be able to fail live.

Why can I see Nora's 76% here but not on Readiness?

Because you are looking as a squadmate. The town cannot. That rule is load-bearing.
